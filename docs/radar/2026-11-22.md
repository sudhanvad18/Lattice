### GLiNER2.5-small local extractor (https://huggingface.co/fastino/gliner2.5-small-v1)

- What: Replace or sit beside the rule-based extractor with Fastino's GLiNER2.5, which does zero-shot NER **and** joint relation extraction locally (CPU/MPS), with a 74M small checkpoint for laptops.
- Fit: Lattice / `packages/core/lattice/graph/extractor.py` and `packages/core/lattice/graph/builder.py`. Also improves item 4 (cleaner spans before merge) and the aerospace/regulatory long shot below.
- Evidence: Official Fastino blog, 24 August 2026, releases three Apache-2.0 checkpoints: `gliner2.5-small-v1` (74M, DeBERTa-v3-xsmall), `gliner2.5-base-v1` (194M), `gliner2.5-multi-v1` (287M). Hugging Face cards confirm `license: apache-2.0`, local inference via `gliner2[local]`, joint relations (`enable_relations=True` on base), and a 4096-token window. Fastino reports GLiNER2.5 Base at 55.14 F1 on Few-NERD vs 47.22 for GLiNER2 Base, and a 24.75-point XNLI gain for the multilingual model (62.30 vs 37.55). The 74M small model is the right first laptop checkpoint; base is the quality comparison. Community discussion on X includes a 27 August 2026 in-browser ONNX port ([@nicolasembleton](https://x.com/nicolasembleton/status/2092971973274194057)) and mid-November notes from [@fastinoAI](https://x.com/fastinoAI) (verified) about production use. One independent calibration measurement on 2,197 bounded-extraction rows should be treated as a single-evaluator result, not a benchmark: 32.6% accuracy / 0.302 ECE for Base vs 24.9% / 0.060 ECE for Small ([@trashhalo, 24 September 2026](https://x.com/trashhalo/status/2103136054761169018)).
- Experiment: write a `GLiNERExtractor` behind the existing `extract()` interface, schema-aligned to Lattice types used on `examples/aerospace` (SPEC, PART, PROCEDURE, MATERIAL, CONDITION, plus relations such as REFERENCES / REQUIRES). Compare entity/relation counts and a 20-fact precision review against `RuleBasedExtractor` on that corpus. Keep the rule-based path as default until the spike beats it on that review. Start with `fastino/gliner2.5-small-v1`; only move to base if quality needs it.
- Effort: M.

### Lightfoot-style provenance and exact delete (https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.11010)

- What: Steal two operational ideas from the Lightfoot GraphRAG paper (7 November 2026), not necessarily the whole library: every graph fact keeps chunk-level provenance, and deleting a document is an exact, model-free transaction that matches a fresh index of the remaining corpus.
- Fit: Lattice / `packages/core/lattice/graph/builder.py`, `packages/core/lattice/knowledge_base.py` (`reset` and `delete` paths), and item 4 (the paper's synonym-edge discussion is about near-duplicate names). Lattice already stores `source_chunk_ids` on entities/relations — the gap is whether deletion and re-ingest stay exact.
- Evidence: arXiv:2611.11010, submitted 7 November 2026. Under identical extraction/embedding/answer models, the authors report Lightfoot beating HippoRAG 2 on MuSiQue recall@5 / @10 by +4.8 / +5.5 points (Holm-corrected paired tests) and remaining exact after deleting 10% of an 11,402-passage corpus (14 seconds, no model calls), while HippoRAG 2's capped synonym lists were 565 edges short of a rebuild on a near-duplicate-name corpus. The GitHub repo [udayallu-ml/Lightfoot-GraphRAG](https://github.com/udayallu-ml/Lightfoot-GraphRAG) exists (MIT, created 7 November 2026) but had only 1 star and a single push when checked; treat the **paper** as the source of ideas, and the code as unverified for production use. Query-time retrieval with no LLM call also matches Lattice's offline/Ollama constraint better than Microsoft GraphRAG 3.3.0 (8 October 2026) or LightRAG, which the same paper tabulates as requiring model calls at query or delete time.
- Experiment: write a test that ingests two aerospace docs, deletes one, and asserts the remaining graph equals a fresh ingest of the survivor (entity/relation identity, not just counts). Optionally add synonym edges (not merges) for near-duplicate names that fail the Adopt resolver, and measure retrieval recall on a handful of multi-hop questions.
- Effort: M.

### 5. Cross-PDE one-step probe on The Well (new project, grounded in a 2026 dataset paper)

- **Who it is for:** the MeanFlow research program, especially the gap "cross-PDE class conditioning / mixed-family dataset" and `label_dim=0` ("one checkpoint belongs to one experiment").
- **Why now:** The Well ([arXiv:2412.00568](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.00568)) is a 15 TB, 16-domain PDE benchmark. [arXiv:2610.33967](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.33967) (submitted 25 October 2026) shows that modest TFNO tuning, even with one input timestep and the same 12-hour budget, beat published FNO/TFNO baselines on 15 of 17 Well tasks — so any new comparison must retune, not copy the released numbers. That is the right moment to ask whether an improved-MeanFlow + class label probe can sit beside a carefully tuned operator baseline on *one* 2D Well task.
- **First experiment:** pick one 2D Well task, train a small model in a **new directory** (not the publication checkout) for a laptop-or-single-GPU budget, and report VRMSE / Energy Score against a retuned TFNO under the same wall clock. No ALCF job in week one.

### 6. Physics-informed conformal calibration of MeanFlow ensembles (new project)

- **Who it is for:** anyone reporting q90/q95 intervals from MeanFlow ensembles, including future paper text.
- **Why now:** `CODEBASE_CONTEXT.md` is explicit that current intervals are empirical ensemble quantiles, not conformal calibration, and that coverage remains imperfect. [arXiv:2609.11935](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.11935) ("Physics-Informed Conformal Prediction…"), submitted 26 June 2026 and revised **16 November 2026 (v2)**, proposes PI-CP (PDE residuals in the nonconformity score) and reports 89–91% coverage across six physics scenarios in that paper's setting. That is a concrete method to try on the existing K=256 protocol, not a reason to cite those coverage numbers as MeanFlow results.
- **First experiment:** on the frozen 500-case validation protocol, use 400 cases to calibrate and 100 to check coverage of current empirical intervals vs a residual-augmented nonconformity score, in physical units. Compare to the already-recorded "coverage remains imperfect" qualitative claim.
- **First week:** a notebook or script beside the checkout that calls the existing evaluator; no changes to sealed inference configs; a short coverage table.
- **Reused code:** `evaluate_ensemble_calibration.py` / `tools/paired_pde` (read-only invocation).
