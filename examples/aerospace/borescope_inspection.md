# Borescope Inspection Procedure

This is a fictional training procedure for the TF-40 demonstration corpus. Actual access, cooling periods, rotation methods, and acceptance limits come from the approved engine maintenance manual. Borescope Inspection requires Engine Isolation. Engine Isolation requires Maintenance Authorization. Inspection findings support an engineering decision; an image alone does not establish that an engine is safe to return to service.

## Preparation and access

Review the work scope, previous images, and the suspected location before opening an access port. Verify engine identity against the work order. Account for inspection tools and loose items. Confirm that the engine has reached the permitted inspection condition and that ignition and rotation hazards have been controlled. Use only the approved rotor turning method and maintain communication with the person controlling rotation.

Borescope Inspection requires Calibrated Equipment. Probe Calibration requires Reference Target. Verify image focus, illumination, measurement mode, and storage before entering the engine. Document the probe identifier and calibration status. Protect the probe from sharp access edges. If contamination obscures the view, follow the approved cleaning process; increasing image contrast cannot recover a hidden surface feature.

## Systematic coverage

Inspect each accessible stage in the sequence defined by the work card. Record the access port, stage, blade position, viewing direction, and inspection coverage. Borescope Inspection detects Thermal Fatigue. Thermal Fatigue causes Surface Cracking. Inspect blade edges, platforms, accessible roots, and surrounding stationary structure where the scope permits. Identify reflection, coating texture, and deposits before describing a line as a crack.

For each indication, capture an overview and a close view with enough context to relocate the feature. Measurement uncertainty increases when the probe angle is poor or the reference surface is unclear. Record that limitation explicitly. Do not convert a two-dimensional image into a crack depth estimate without the approved measurement method. Obtain a second view when the original geometry makes interpretation uncertain.

## Disposition and closure

Inspection Report requires Image Traceability. Link every retained image to the work order and describe areas that could not be inspected. Compare findings with the current manual revision, not an old photograph's informal label. Escalate uncertain or out-of-limit findings to the authorized engineering process. After probe withdrawal, account for equipment and restore access hardware using the specified instructions. Independent verification, where required by the maintenance system, closes the inspection record. Preserve both acceptable observations and defects so future reviews can evaluate change over time.
