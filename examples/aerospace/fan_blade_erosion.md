# Fan Blade Erosion

This training document describes a fictional TF-40 turbofan fleet. Its intervals and inspection criteria are illustrative; the applicable engine maintenance manual controls actual maintenance decisions. Fan Blade Erosion causes Aerodynamic Efficiency Loss. Sand Ingestion causes Fan Blade Erosion. Leading Edge Inspection requires Borescope Inspection. These relationships connect operational observations to inspection work, rather than assigning a serviceability limit from appearance alone.

## Exposure and symptoms

Fine mineral particles strike the fan leading edge during operation from unpaved or dusty locations. Repeated impacts round the original contour and can remove a protective surface finish. The change may appear as a polished band before measurable loss becomes obvious. Salt deposits and retained moisture complicate the surface assessment. An inspector distinguishes deposited material from missing parent material before recording a defect.

Fan Blade requires Leading Edge Inspection. Blade Vibration causes Attachment Wear. A trend in fuel consumption, corrected for ambient conditions and operating point, can support an investigation but does not identify erosion uniquely. Intake distortion, contamination, and sensor drift can produce similar observations. The maintenance record therefore includes operating environment, recent washing, photographs, and blade position.

## Inspection and recording

Clean the accessible surface using the approved process before taking measurements. Record the blade serial number and the radial location of each measurement. Use consistent lighting and image scale so later comparisons do not mistake a shadow for a change in contour. Note dents, folds, cracks, and missing material separately. Erosion Assessment requires Profile Measurement. Profile Measurement requires Calibrated Equipment.

In this fictional fleet, a review occurs after 500 flight cycles in ordinary service and after a significant dust exposure event. This is a planning example, not an approved inspection interval. The responsible engineer compares findings with the current engine manual and checks for related service instructions. Evidence of cracking follows the manual's crack disposition process rather than an erosion blending allowance.

## Material and follow-up

Titanium Fan Blade requires Material Traceability. Replacement stock is identified as Ti-6Al-4V per AMS-4928 in the example procurement record. The procurement specification alone does not authorize machining, blending, substitution, or installation. Confirm the actual part drawing, manufacturing condition, and approved repair scheme. Preserve the original inspection images with the work order. A repeat inspection should compare the same blade locations and distinguish measurement uncertainty from a physical wear trend.
