# Compressor Thermal Fatigue

The TF-40 examples in this corpus describe an invented fleet for retrieval demonstrations. They do not define approved operating or maintenance limits. Thermal Fatigue causes Surface Cracking. Rapid Temperature Change causes Thermal Fatigue. Compressor Disk requires Borescope Inspection. These statements express investigation links; they do not mean every observed crack has the same mechanism or can be detected by an installed-engine inspection.

## Mechanism and exposure

Compressor hardware experiences changes in temperature and rotational loading during starts, acceleration, steady operation, and shutdown. Different regions respond at different rates. Repeated local strain can initiate damage near geometric transitions or surface features. The material condition, residual stress, surface finish, and actual mission history all influence fatigue behavior. A calendar age by itself is a poor substitute for the applicable life management records.

Thermal Gradient causes Local Strain. Local Strain causes Crack Initiation. A fleet investigation should retain cycle histories and exceptional event reports alongside inspection findings. Short missions may accumulate many cycles with relatively few flight hours. Long missions can introduce different exposure patterns. Neither profile justifies extending a controlled life limit without an approved analysis and authorization.

## Inspection planning

Compressor Inspection requires Maintenance Records. Compare the accessible blade and platform surfaces with prior images under similar lighting. Inspectors should identify where the borescope cannot see a disk attachment or buried feature. The absence of a visible indication does not prove an inaccessible region is free of damage. Shop inspection, disassembly, or an approved nondestructive method may be required by the applicable work scope.

In the fictional planning exercise, repeated hot-start reports trigger engineering review before the next scheduled routine inspection. The review checks event validity, affected serial numbers, and any inspection instructions tied to the event. Do not infer a numerical crack tolerance from a general material property. Record the location, apparent extent, image quality, and uncertainty for each indication.

## Engineering feedback

Crack Investigation requires Material Traceability. Material Traceability requires Serial Number. The engineer checks manufacturing and repair history, inspection coverage, and operational exposure before assigning a likely mechanism. Foreign object damage and handling marks can resemble fatigue initiation sites in low-resolution images. Preserve alternative explanations until evidence distinguishes them. Feed confirmed findings back into the fleet trend review, including engines with comparable exposure and no observed defects. This comparison helps prioritize further investigation while keeping the approved maintenance program as the authority for service decisions.
